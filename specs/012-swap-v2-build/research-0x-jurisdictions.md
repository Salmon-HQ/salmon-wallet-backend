## 1. Executive summary

1. **0x has no list of prohibited countries and no US exclusion.** Neither the API License Agreement (the contract Salmon signs when creating a dashboard app) nor the site ToS names a single country. The only geographic language is the ToS representation that you are "not a citizen or resident of a state, country, territory or other jurisdiction that is embargoed by the Cayman Islands or the United States" plus a general "comply with all applicable laws" duty.
2. **US position:** 0x (ZeroEx Inc.) is a US company (Delaware/San Francisco per ToS §13/ALA §14 and the enterprise page), governed by California law, and markets a "U.S. based compliance posture". It sells the Swap API to US integrators (Coinbase case study on the site). Nothing in its terms restricts US integrators or US end users. Contrast Jupiter, whose Terms of Use say it "does not interact with digital wallets located in ... the United States".
3. **Restricted persons under 0x = OFAC-listed / OFAC-prohibited persons and PEPs**, not residents of any named country. ALA §8.2(b) makes Salmon warrant that none of Salmon, its affiliates, beneficial owners, or "Licensee Users" is "named on an OFAC list ... or a person or entity prohibited under the OFAC Programs, regardless of whether or not they appear on the OFAC list".
4. **Who bears the obligation:** the _integrator_ (Licensee) carries the compliance representation (ALA §8.2(a)–(b)), the indemnity for "any Licensee User" (ALA §10), and the duty to disclose the 0x Swap Fee to users (ALA §7.1(d)). 0x itself enforces **wallet-address screening** technically. There is **no contractual duty on the integrator to geo-block or to run its own wallet screening** (unlike Jupiter §7.3 and OKX §5.3).
5. **What 0x enforces technically:** every `taker` (and buy/sell token) is screened. Blocked taker → HTTP `403 TAKER_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_TRADE`; blocked token → `422 BUY_TOKEN_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_TRADE` / `SELL_TOKEN_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_TRADE` ("not authorized for trade due to legal restrictions"). Screening is "via CipherOwl against OFAC, EU, UK, and UN lists ... with no opt-out, no extra cost". It is address-based, not IP/country-based; no country-level error code exists. Documented for EVM Swap and Gasless; the Solana `swap-instructions` reference lists no error codes (**Solana error behaviour UNVERIFIED** from docs — needs an empirical test).
6. **API key:** dashboard signup is name + email + password + team name + project type (docs quickstart). No KYC / identity attestation is documented. **UNVERIFIED** whether a country field or a terms checkbox naming the ALA exists on the live form (dashboard returned 403 to fetch); the ALA preamble says you accept it "when signing up for and selecting a plan".
7. **Settlement:** the user signs and broadcasts. 0x docs: "The transaction must be signed by the taker (payer of the swap)". The ALA licenses the _API and ZeroEx Data_ only; the on-chain Settler program is open source (Apache 2.0 per ToS §4) and no separate on-chain terms attach to it. The ToS says 0x "has no oversight, involvement, or control with respect to your transactions".
8. **Fees:** taking an integrator fee (`swap_fee_ppm` / `swap_fee_recipient`) is expressly supported; no jurisdiction-dependent fee term exists. The 0x-side 0.15% fee applies "on swaps involving select token pairs" on the Standard plan; Solana was announced with "zero swap fees" during beta.
9. **Privacy/IP:** 0x's notice covers IP/log data of whoever hits its Services. Because the backend, not the device, calls 0x, 0x sees Salmon's server IP and the `taker` address only. The ALA has no clause requiring the integrator to forward end-user IPs and no clause forbidding it.
10. **Practical answer for the region gate:** 0x's own terms let Salmon serve every territory except OFAC-embargoed ones; wallet sanctions screening is done by 0x. The US question therefore moves from "does the provider allow it" (Jupiter: no) to "does Salmon's own regulatory posture allow it" (counsel question, §6).

### 2.3 "Restricted Person" definition

No defined term "Restricted Person" or "Prohibited Jurisdiction" exists in either 0x document. The functional equivalent is ALA §8.2(b):

"**Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Crimes Compliance.** Any funds that are or will be used by Licensee or Licensee Users for trading were not and are not directly or indirectly derived from any activities that contravene any law, rule, regulation or order (including anti-money laundering laws and regulations) applicable to Licensee. None of: (a) Licensee; (b) any Affiliate of Licensee; (c) any person having a beneficial interest in Licensee; (d) any person for whom Licensee is acting as agent or nominee in connection with this Agreement; or (e) Licensee Users is: (i) a country, territory, entity or individual named on an OFAC list as provided at http://www.treas.gov/ofac, or a person or entity prohibited under the OFAC Programs, regardless of whether or not they appear on the OFAC list; or (ii) a senior foreign political figure, or any immediate family member or close associate of a senior foreign political figure."

Note the reach: "(e) Licensee Users" makes Salmon warrant that **none of its end users** is OFAC-listed/prohibited or a PEP. OFAC "Programs" include the country-based comprehensive programs (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea/DNR/LNR) — so residents of those territories are "prohibited under the OFAC Programs" even if not individually listed. This is the only place a "country" restriction is implied. The PEP prong (ii) is unusual and is not something wallet screening can satisfy — see §6.

## 3. Mechanics

### (a) Quote / instruction build by a restricted person

- **Sanctioned wallet:** technical refusal by 0x — `403 TAKER_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_TRADE` (documented for EVM Swap + Gasless; Solana **UNVERIFIED** but stated as universal). Salmon does not have to detect it first; it must not circumvent it (ToS §2(vi)) and must surface it (AGENTS.md error-envelope rule: pass a specific code, never a fake 200).
- **Sanctioned token:** `422 *_TOKEN_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_TRADE`.
- **User in an embargoed country with a clean wallet:** 0x cannot see it (no IP forwarded) and has no country check. There is **no technical refusal**; the exposure is contractual/legal: Salmon's ALA §8.2(b) warranty that no Licensee User is "prohibited under the OFAC Programs", plus §8.2(a) "comply with all applicable laws", plus indemnity §10(v). Whether that warranty is breached by a Cuban/Iranian resident using Salmon depends on OFAC law, not on 0x — counsel.
- **US user with a clean wallet:** nothing in 0x's terms is breached. Any restriction is Salmon's own regulatory choice.
- **Consequences of breach under the ALA:** suspension/termination at ZeroEx's discretion (§5.1), indemnity (§10), 30-day cure for material breach (§11.2.1).

### (b) Signing and broadcast

- Docs (get-started): "The transaction must be signed by the **taker** (payer of the swap)"; the client calls `sendTransaction` itself. Open-beta post: users "maintain full control over transaction submission, priority fees, and compute budgets rather than relying on 0x to broadcast transactions."
- Integration notes: the response contains "A single instruction on our **Settler** program to perform the actual swap" (https://github.com/0xProject/0x-settler). The ALA's licence covers "the API ... and the accompanying Documentation" and "ZeroEx Data" (§3.1) — it says nothing about the on-chain program. ToS §4: "Certain of the Services are governed by the most recent version of the open source licenses, including the Apache 2.0 license". ToS §8: "ZeroEx does not operate a virtual currency or derivatives exchange platform or offer trade execution or clearing services and therefore has no oversight, involvement, or control with respect to your transactions."
- Net: **0x's terms attach to API access, not to on-chain settlement.** No term prohibits a user from signing/broadcasting a returned transaction from any location. The only on-chain hook is the fee/surplus routing the instructions encode. (**UNVERIFIED**: whether the Solana Settler program has any on-chain allowlist/blocklist — the Halborn audit exists at halborn.com but was not read; the EVM Settler is permissionless per its README.)

### (c) Fee collection

- Taking `swap_fee_ppm` does not change any jurisdiction term — the ALA has none. It does make Salmon a fee-earning party on every swap, which is the fact counsel will care about for money-transmission / broker-style analysis in the US and for MiCA "crypto-asset service" analysis in the EU. 0x's own position on itself (enterprise page): "0x is a technology provider with non-custodial routing and settlement. Funds move wallet-to-wallet through audited contracts and never touch us, so there is no money-transmitter posture for you to inherit." That is 0x's view of 0x, not a statement about Salmon.
- ALA §7.1(d) obliges Salmon to disclose the **0x** swap fee to users; nothing obliges disclosure of Salmon's own fee, but consumer law may.

## 6. Open questions for counsel

1. **US:** 0x permits US users; does Salmon (non-custodial, fee-taking, backend-built unsigned tx) have any US money-transmission / broker / state-law exposure that 0x's "no money-transmitter posture for you to inherit" claim does not cover? Does the Apple 3.1.5 review history change the answer for iOS?
2. **ALA §8.2(b)(e):** how strong is the warranty that no "Licensee User" is OFAC-prohibited or a **PEP**? Does 0x's own CipherOwl screening discharge it, or does Salmon need its own screening/attestation (and does the PEP prong require anything at all for a non-custodial wallet)?
3. **Country vs wallet:** OFAC comprehensive programs restrict _persons in_ Cuba/Iran/NK/Syria/Crimea, not only listed wallets. With no IP forwarded to 0x, is Salmon's server-side country gate (by requester IP / app store region) sufficient "reasonable" compliance? Which signal (IP, SIM, store region, self-attestation) should the gate use?
4. **Precedence:** ALA §14.1 ("to the exclusion of all other terms") vs ToS §14 ("these Terms will control") — which document governs an API dispute, and does the ToS's Cayman-embargo representation bind an API licensee?
5. **Partial-sanctions programs** (Venezuela, Russia, Belarus, Nicaragua, Myanmar, etc.): 0x's terms are silent; OFAC is not. Which of these must the gate block for 0x?
6. **Fee disclosure:** ALA §7.1(d) requires disclosing the 0x Swap Fee "prior to each Licensee User's use". What UI text satisfies this, and does Salmon's own fee need equivalent disclosure under consumer law in EU/UK/LatAm?
7. **MiCA / UK FCA / LatAm VASP regimes:** does a fee-taking swap feature in a non-custodial wallet constitute a crypto-asset service (EU), a regulated activity (UK), or a VASP activity (AR/BR/MX)? Provider terms do not answer this.
8. **Jupiter fallback:** if Jupiter remains in the routing table, License §7.3 (wallet screening "in accordance with prevailing best market practice") and §8.4 ("Powered by Jupiter") still bind Salmon. Is it worth keeping given the added duties?
9. **On-chain:** confirm with 0x support whether the Solana Settler program or the `swap-instructions` endpoint applies any taker/country restriction beyond CipherOwl, and get the Solana error code for a blocked taker in writing.
10. **Data:** since Salmon proxies, 0x sees only the `taker` address. Confirm no contractual expectation that Salmon forwards user IP or geodata, and confirm Salmon's own privacy policy covers sharing the wallet address with 0x/CipherOwl (EEA/UK users).
